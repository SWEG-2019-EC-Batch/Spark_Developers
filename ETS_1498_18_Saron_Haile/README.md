  IPO of student course total
  
A. Input:
- assignment_mark
- midterm_mark 
- final_exam_mark 
Output:
- total_mark 
- Pass/Fail status

B. Process
1. Start the program.
2. Declare the required double variables.
3. Read the assignment, midterm, and final exam marks from the user.
4. Calculate assignment_weighted_mark by multiplying assignment_mark by 20 and dividing by 100.
5. Calculate midterm_weighted_mark by multiplying midterm_mark by 30 and dividing by 100.
6. Calculate final_exam_weighted_mark by multiplying final_exam_mark by 50 and dividing by 100.
7. Calculate total_mark by adding the three weighted marks.
8. Display total_mark to one decimal place.
9. Use the conditional operator to display Pass if total_mark is greater than or equal to 50; otherwise, display Fail.
10. End the program.

    IPO of electric bill
    
A. Inputs:
- units 
Outputs:
- energy_charge 
- service_charge_value 
- total_bill

  B. Process:
1. Start the program.
2. Declare an integer variable units to store the electricity units consumed.
3. Declare double variables energy_charge and total_bill, and a constant service_charge_value.
4. Set the service charge to 10 birr.
5. Read the number of electricity units from the user.
6. Calculate the energy charge using the conditional operator:
   - If the units are less than or equal to 50, multiply the units by 1.50 birr.
   - Otherwise, calculate the first 50 units at 1.50 birr each and the remaining units at 2.00 birr each.
7. Calculate the total bill by adding the energy charge and service charge.
8. Display the energy charge, service charge, and total bill, formatted to two decimal places.
9. End the program.

    IPO of trip fuel and fuel cost
   
A. Inputs
- distance
- hours
- minutes
- fuel_consumption
- price_per_liter

   Outputs
- average_speed 
- fuel_used
- fuel_cost

B. Process
1. Start the program.
2. Declare variables for distance, hours, minutes, fuel consumption, fuel price, total time, average speed, fuel used, and fuel cost.
3. Read the trip distance, hours, minutes, fuel consumption, and price per liter from the user.
4. Convert the travel time into hours by dividing the minutes by 60 and adding the hours.
5. Calculate the average speed by dividing the distance by the total travel time.
6. Calculate the fuel used based on the distance and fuel consumption per 100 km.
7. Calculate the fuel cost by multiplying the fuel used by the price per liter.
8. Display the average speed, fuel used, and fuel cost with two decimal places.
9. End the program.
